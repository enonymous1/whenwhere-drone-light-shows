Where these numbers come from

Nothing here is made up. The measurements are read off the show file this preview loaded; the limits come from the FAA and from Skybrush, and the launch spacing is industry practice. The rules live in rules.js in the repo.

  • 120 m ceiling — FAA Part 107, §107.51: a small drone may not fly more than 400 ft (121.92 m) above ground level; higher needs a waiver. See the FAA Part 107 waivers page.
  • Night shows and swarms need waivers — §107.29 (night and civil twilight) and §107.35 (one pilot flying more than one aircraft at a time). A drone light show needs both waived, plus §107.51 if it flies over 400 ft.
  • Safety buffer — 10 m wider for every 100 ft of altitude — FAA waiver framework: a waiver's special provisions widen the restricted / free-fall area as the show climbs. The exact buffer is set per operation in the waiver, so this preview uses 10 m per 100 ft as its baseline. See the FAA Part 107 waivers page.
  • Launch-pad pitch — 2 m — industry practice, not a regulation: swarm drones are spaced apart on the ground so one drone's rotor wash doesn't buffet its neighbour on simultaneous liftoff. A drone-show guide puts the typical ground spacing at 1.5–2 m. Drone light show guide.
  • Takeoff / landing check — 0.4 m outdoors — Skybrush Live 2.9.0+ validates the minimum distance between takeoff positions (and between landing positions) when a show loads, and refuses a show under 0.4 m outdoors (0.2 m indoors). Skybrush Live commit · Skybrush Live 2.9.0 release notes.
  • The measurements themselves — the picture's width and height, its centre altitude and how far above the ground it spans, the takeoff grid, the sightline distances and the camera's distance: all measured from the show file this preview loaded — the actual formation, takeoff grid, and camera position — not from a regulation.
